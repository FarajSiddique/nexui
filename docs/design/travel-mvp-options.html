Three ways to draw a trip

Mobile directions for Nexui's first slice: a travel planner where the trip is an object graph and the workspace is assembled from seven primitives (map, route, metric, object list, comparison, decision, insight). Each direction shows the same five moments: Home, a workspace right after a stop was shortened, the decision that answers "Could we use the extra time somewhere rural?", the + sheet while a run streams objects in, and Changes with Undo.

Every palette is a token set with a light and a dark value. Use the theme control to flip all phones; each option also shows its workspace in the opposite theme. Phone screens scroll.

  • Tabs: Home, +, Changes
  • Workspace pushes over Home
  • Only the seven primitives
  • No tasks or checkboxes
  • AI content looks different from yours
  • Every change has Undo
Phones and page

Option A, evolves the current brandItinerary Page

The workspace reads like one well-kept page, top to bottom: a map postcard, the numbers, what needs attention, then the route as an editable spine. Conversation stays in the + sheet; the page is the record.

Example trips
Japan in December (the workspace), Chicago weekend, and a Portugal road trip Nexui is still drafting.
Brand
Keeps violet-tinted paper, white cards, ink, yellow, Bricolage Grotesque and Atkinson Hyperlegible. The existing highlighter idea becomes the AI mark: anything Nexui wrote sits on a yellow highlighter stroke with a small Nexui tag. Your own objects are plain ink.
Dark mode
Paper goes to a violet-black (#15131B), cards lift to #211E2A. Yellow stays as a solid fill with dark ink (13.3:1) for buttons and the unallocated metric, because it glows on dark. The highlighter cannot stay solid behind light text, so it becomes a 28% yellow wash (light text on it measures about 8:1) and the Nexui tag switches to yellow text on a faint yellow tint (12.9:1). The map route turns yellow on dark. Checked: body text 15–16:1, secondary text 6–8.8:1, the faintest text 4.9:1 (light) and 6.0:1 (dark). All text and controls pass WCAG AA in both themes.
Optimizes for
Learnability and continuity. It looks like the app people already use, and a scrolling page is the cheapest mental model. The order of sections (map, numbers, insight, route, decisions) is the template's default section order, so the renderer is a plain list of primitives.
Risks and tradeoffs
Pages get long as trips grow, so decisions can drift below the fold; this mockup pins open decisions right under the metrics to counter that. The map is a postcard, not a tool: you cannot plan by dragging on it. It is the least surprising of the three, which is also its weakness.
How the + sheet behaves
A tall sheet with a context chip naming the trip. Runs stream objects into the sheet and into the page at the same time, so closing the sheet loses nothing.

Option B, departs from the brandAtlas

The map is the workspace. Every other primitive lives in one bottom sheet that rises over it: peeked for the route, half-open for a decision, tall for +. Places Nexui proposes are pencilled onto the map before you commit to them.

Example trips
A Portugal road trip (the workspace), Chicago weekend, and a beach week that still has an open "How long is the trip?" decision.
Brand, and why it departs
Violet paper fights map colors and makes pins hard to read, so the ground becomes a cool chart palette: navy ink, pale land, sea blue, with the route in transit magenta so it reads as a line you travel. Schibsted Grotesk replaces Bricolage for its compact, sign-like numerals on pins and chips; Atkinson stays for body text. Yellow survives in one job only: the "needs you" signal (the unallocated day). AI content is pencilled: a violet dashed outline and dashed pins. Your own objects are solid ink.
Dark mode
The map goes to a night chart (land #1A2839, sea #0A1420). Magenta is lifted to #FF5FA2 so the route keeps 5.3:1 against dark land; the violet pencil lifts to #B9A1FF (7.3:1). The primary button inverts to pale ink on navy text. Yellow never carries light text: it keeps navy ink in both themes (12:1). Checked: body 13.6–15.3:1, secondary 5.7–8.2:1. All text and controls pass WCAG AA in both themes.
Optimizes for
Spatial sense and delight. Travel is geography: you see at once that a detour is short or that a stop is out on a limb. Choosing a candidate on the map and in the sheet is the same act.
Risks and tradeoffs
The most expensive to build and the least portable. It needs native maps, a gesture-driven sheet with snap points and pins kept in sync with cards, and web gets a list fallback anyway. It also scales worst to job search: a pipeline has no geography, so the spatial model has to be swapped out, and the shell would feel like two apps.
How the + sheet behaves
The same sheet changes mode. While a run streams, new places drop onto the map as dashed pins and appear as rows below the question.

Option C, departs from the brandDecision Board

Every workspace is split into what is open and what is settled. Open calls (decisions, insights) sit on top as big tiles; the settled trip (day bar, map, route) is a compact ledger below. Each object is a tile colored by its kind, so the graph itself is visible.

Example trips
Southeast Asia on a backpack (the workspace), Chicago weekend, and a beach week waiting on "How long is the trip?".
Brand, and why it departs
It keeps the brand's existing marker colors (mint, sky, yellow, peach, pink) but promotes them to kind colors: place, leg, decision, insight, option. That only works on a neutral cool-grey board, so violet paper goes. Archivo at 125% width replaces Bricolage for numbers and questions that must stay short on narrow tiles. AI content gets a hatched top edge and a solid Nexui tag, like a draft stamped on a card; your own tiles are clean.
Dark mode
Pastel fills would glare on a dark board, so each kind color becomes a deep tint with a light ink of the same hue (mint #1E4536 with #B6F3D4, 8.6:1; peach #4D2E17 with #FFD3B0, 8.8:1). Decisions are the one exception: they stay solid yellow with dark ink (14.2:1) in both themes, so the open call is the brightest thing on the board. The hatch switches from dark lines to light lines. Checked: body 14.5–16.1:1, secondary 5.6–8.0:1, all kind pairs 8.8:1 or better. All text and controls pass WCAG AA in both themes.
Optimizes for
Getting to the next call fast, and scaling to slice 2. Home becomes a list of calls across all intents, not a list of trips. Job search maps directly: a pipeline is a set of settled tiles, and "Which offer?" is the same yellow decision tile.
Risks and tradeoffs
Least spatial: the map is a small tile, not a place to plan. Kind colors are a small vocabulary to learn (a legend helps at first). Many colored tiles can look busy; the design depends on the discipline of one yellow thing at a time. "Open" must never read as a to-do list, so tiles hold questions and objects, never "Do X".
How the + sheet behaves
The sheet shows the board lane the results will land in. New tiles arrive hatched and settle into Open; the decision tile appears once all options are in.

Recommendation

Build A for slice 1, and borrow two things from C. A is the cheapest to ship, closest to the brand, and its section list maps one-to-one onto the workspace doc, so the renderer stays trivial. From C, take the pinned Open area above the route and the proportional day bar, since "1 day free" is easier to see as a gap than as a number.

Keep B's pencil-versus-ink idea as the fallback AI cue under the highlighter (dashed outlines survive grayscale and colorblindness). Hold off on B's full-screen map until slice 1 has proven the graph. Of the three it costs the most to build and carries over worst to job search.
